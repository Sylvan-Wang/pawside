## 4. 帖子正文（英文）

**标题**

> My gym app's plan only moves forward when you actually train. Here's why I dropped the calendar.

**正文**

> I'm building Pawside, a small web app for people who are new to lifting. One decision shaped most of it, and I'd like to hear if you think it's wrong.
>
> **Most workout plans are calendars.** Monday is chest, Wednesday is back, Friday is legs. Miss Wednesday and the app now shows you as behind. Miss a week and the whole plan looks broken. Talking to beginners, this was the point where most of them stopped: not when it got hard, but when the plan started to feel like a list of things they'd failed.
>
> **So the plan in Pawside is a queue, not a calendar.** You pick a split once (push / pull / legs, or a 4-day split). Each time you open the app, it shows the next day in the rotation, with the exercises and the target for each set. Train today, it moves on. Skip three days, it's still waiting at the same place. The weekly goal on the home screen is a number you set yourself, and a rest day doesn't count as a miss.
>
> **What this cost us:**
> - No "streak" pressure, which is a cheap way to bring people back. We decided the guilt wasn't worth it.
> - Harder to say "you're on week 6 of 12." A queue has no end date.
> - Some people *want* a fixed schedule. For them the calendar is a feature.
>
> **What else is in it today:** logging each set with one tap (kg or lb), a short written note after each session, and basic meal logging with rough calories and protein.
>
> **Caveat:** it started as a Chinese-language app. The English version is new, and the written notes after a session are still Chinese only.
>
> Two questions for anyone who's built or used a habit/fitness app:
> 1. Did removing the calendar ever backfire for you?
> 2. If you lift: what made you stop the last time you stopped?
>
